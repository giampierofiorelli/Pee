## Part 0 — Protocol

### 0.1 PEE requirements used

These are taken from the owner's directive:

- distinguish mechanism, engineering implementation and measured outcome;
- preserve numbers, units, conditions, uncertainty, the tested object, the method and comparability;
- represent expert judgement with its holder, criteria, use case and competence, without turning it into measurement;
- distinguish functions from performance characteristics;
- keep provenance separate: manufacturer, independent measurement, expert, method assumption, system-derived;
- recognise genuinely equivalent terminology without merging distinct concepts, metrics, methods or technologies,
  and keep the relations between them;
- reuse knowledge across documents and categories only under stated conditions;
- connect knowledge to ordinary user goals, separating essential constraints from preferences, and including
  non-product solutions;
- avoid unsupported conclusions, invalid comparisons, inappropriate transfers and unjustified certainty.

**Operational standard:**

- coexisting definitions are not prematurely identified;
- genuinely equivalent measurements are recognised as comparable;
- uncertainty is preserved, but indeterminacy is not a universal escape;
- **an incorrect merger is worse than a justified temporary separation**;
- unknown facts are not turned into negative findings;
- contradictory expert interpretations keep their attribution;
- a mechanism is never confused with an outcome.

### 0.2 What can and cannot be differentiated

S0 2.0 and S0 2.1 are **identical except for**:

- D4 (anchors of own instances);
- D5-b (requirement condition, admissible granularity);
- section 3.6 (identity procedure);
- T3, FD5, the lineage table, 8.16, FC7–FC8 and O1/O11.

Hypothesis fixed in advance: **differences between the two encodings outside identity-dependent tasks (F, and the
concept-alignment part of G) are encoder variance, not version effects.** They are reported as such, never as an
advantage of either version.

### 0.4 Roles and separation

1. **Preparer** (a Claude instance with web access, which does not read the specifications or this report). For each
   document it writes a **neutral pack**: exact identity, URL or DOI, access level, evidence type, and the relevant
   sections, with the document's statements paraphrased, the key figures and conditions, and short quotations (25
   words or fewer). Packs contain no S0 terminology and no interpretation beyond what the document says. No full texts
   are stored.
2. **Reference annotator** (a separate Claude instance with web access, which reads neither the specifications nor the
   packs). It reads the original documents and writes **source-grounded reference answers** to the tasks of 0.5,
   recording what each source actually establishes, with its limits and the errors to avoid. The reference is
   **hidden** until both encodings are fixed.
3. **Encoder 2.0** and **Encoder 2.1** (two Claude instances). Each reads only its version of the specification
   (exported from `b9bda7d` or `cdcab48`, without chapter 8) and the packs, and fills in the same template (0.6). Neither
   sees the other version, the reference or `reviews/`.
4. **Designer.** After the encodings are fixed and before reading the reference, the designer independently
   re-encodes the identity-dependent items (F and the alignment parts of G) under **both** versions, to measure
   determinacy. The designer then scores everything against the reference.

### 0.5 Tasks

Per document (D = S1…S8):

- **A. Mechanism / implementation / outcome.** List the physical or chemical mechanisms, their engineering
  implementation and the measured outcomes, kept distinct.
- **B. Quantitative.** Every reported result, with value, unit, conditions, uncertainty or dispersion, tested object,
  method and comparability limits.
- **C. Qualitative.** Every expert or panel judgement, with holder, criteria, use case and competence. Kept distinct
  from measurements.
- **D. Functions versus performance**, where applicable.
- **E. Provenance.** Each claim tagged as manufacturer, independent measurement, expert, method assumption, or
  system-derived.

Across documents:

- **F. Identity.**
  - F1: candidate equivalent or near-equivalent terms, metrics and methods found within or across the packs. The
    encoders list them, and the reference lists those the sources support. Each pair gets its identity verdict, the
    extension relation and the structural relations, **by the encoder's version of section 3.6**, plus any alternative
    faithful reading.
  - F2: which measurements across documents are comparable, and under what conditions.
- **G. Cross-document learning.**
  - G1: S1 → S6 (dehumidifier → heat-pump dryer).
  - G2: S2 → S8 (research on purifiers → manufacturer claims).
  - G3: S4 → S7 (laundry detergency → dishwashing).

  For each: what transfers, and under which conditions (TC1–TC5); what must not transfer.
- **H. User goals.** For each need, list the essential constraints, the preferences, the relevant knowledge from the
  packs, the evidence still missing, the inferences that are not allowed, and non-product solutions:
  - H1: reduce condensation in a cold home;
  - H2: remove a given stain without damaging the fabric;
  - H3: good espresso by relevant specialist standards;
  - H4: limited space, and no plumbing modifications possible (dishwashing or drying);
  - H5: prefer quiet operation, without confusing a night mode with measured quietness.
- **I. Decision safety.** List the conclusions, comparisons and transfers that the material does **not** support.

### 0.6 Output template, common to both encoders

For each item:

- `id`; `statement`;
- `S0 kind` (3.4); `plane` (3.5);
- `role` (mechanism / implementation / function / performance / claim / norm / goal);
- `quantity, value, unit, conditions, uncertainty, tested object, method`;
- `holder/provenance`; `standing`;
- `comparability / transfer notes`;
- `alternatives` (other faithful readings).

For F, additionally: `pair`, `step`, `verdict`, `extension relation`, `structural relations`, `alternative verdicts`.

### 0.7 Scoring (fixed now)

Each encoder's output is compared item by item with the reference.

| Code | Category | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| P | relevant fact or relation correctly preserved | — |
| L | relevant information lost, or left unusably indeterminate | major if it blocks a needed comparison or need; otherwise minor |
| U | unsupported addition or incorrect scientific inference | **critical** if it would change a decision; otherwise major |
| AT | attribution or tested-product error (e.g. a manufacturer claim treated as an independent measurement) | **critical** |
| Q | lost condition, qualification, unit or uncertainty | major |
| M | incorrect merger of concepts, metrics, methods or technologies | **critical** |
| S | incorrect separation (equivalence missed where the sources support it) | major |
| IA / II | appropriate / inappropriate indeterminacy | II is major |
| T+ / T− | correctly licensed / unsupported cross-category transfer | T− is **critical** |
| MO | mechanism confused with outcome, or function with performance | **critical** |
| UN | unknown turned into a negative finding | **critical** |
| IC | identity inconsistency (non-transitive "same" chain, contradictory verdicts) | **critical** |

A good average never offsets a critical error. Results are reported as counts on a small sample, never as general
precision or recall.

**Determinacy.** For F and G-alignment, the share of items where the designer's independent re-encoding gives the same
verdict as the encoder of that version.

### 0.8 Analysis of identity properties (by the designer, analytical)

For each version:

- reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity of "same concept";
- three-concept chains;
- conflation of identity with extension, mechanism, scope, terminology or lineage;
- refuted model versus eliminated concept;
- whether coexisting definitions must be identified for knowledge to be reused;
- recording contested interpretations;
- the risk of excessive indeterminacy and of duplication.

A conservative baseline is analysed alongside: separate concepts, evidence-backed relations, identity only provisional.
This baseline is not adopted.

### 0.9 Decision rule for the comparison

- **One version preferred** if it has fewer critical errors attributable to version-specific rules, and no more
  inappropriate indeterminacy or identity inconsistencies.
- **"Insufficient in different ways"** if each is worse on a different critical dimension.
- **"Operationally indistinguishable"** if the version-specific differences produce no difference in critical or major
  counts.

The final recommendation answers the owner's eight questions (section 10 of the directive) and states its uncertainty.

### 0.10 What can be concluded

1. Can Claude interpret the text? (Investigated.)
2. Can S0 2.0 or 2.1 faithfully represent that interpretation? (Investigated.)
3. Has an autonomous PEE learned and reused the knowledge? (**Not investigated. Untested.**)

## Part 4 — Identity properties (analytical) [INT]

This analysis is of the written rules: section 3.6 at `b9bda7d` for 2.0, and at `cdcab48` for 2.1. It does not depend
on the document encodings.

### 4.1 Is "same concept" an equivalence relation?

| Property | S0 2.0 | S0 2.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Reflexive | yes | yes |
| Symmetric | yes for coexisting concepts. The lineage verdict is directional, but "same concept" is read symmetrically | yes. Inclusion in either direction is symmetric |
| Transitive (coexisting) | **Depends on an unresolved reading.** If "shared goals" and "shared paradigm anchors" mean *equal*, the goal and anchor parts are transitive. But "agreeing extension on the shared domain" is relative to each pair's shared domain, so transitivity can still fail. If "shared" means *overlapping*, transitivity fails outright. The text does not say which reading holds, and this was one source of disagreement between encoders in the 2.0 run | **Not transitive.** Goal inclusion in either direction is not transitive (4.2) |
| Transitive (lineage) | **Not transitive.** Anchor continuity tolerates reclassification of individual exemplars, so a chain of small steps can end with a different body of paradigm cases (a sorites) | **Not transitive**, for the same reason. 2.1 states partial continuity explicitly |

**Finding.** In **neither** version is "same concept" guaranteed to be an equivalence relation. In 2.1 the failure is
structural (4.2). In 2.0 it depends on how the text is read.

### 4.2 Three-concept chain under 2.1

Take three coexisting concepts with the same strong paradigm anchors (A is preserved for every pair), and with goal
sets that are stable at every admissible level:

- G(B) = {g}
- G(A) = {g, h}
- G(C) = {g, k}

Under 2.1:

- A–B: G(B) ⊆ G(A), so G is preserved and the verdict is **same** (step 5).
- B–C: G(B) ⊆ G(C), so the verdict is **same**.
- A–C: each serves a goal the other lacks, so G is broken, A is preserved, and the verdict is **indeterminate**
  (step 3).

So **A = B and B = C, while A–C is indeterminate.** If a system merged stored knowledge on every "same" verdict, the
knowledge of A and C would be merged through B, against the rule's own verdict on A–C. That is the critical error PEE
must avoid.

**Product-domain instance (illustrative; the design examples are not evidence).**

- B: a generic usage, "how much particulate an air purifier removes".
- A: CADR, which adds the goal of comparing devices by room size.
- C: single-pass filter efficiency (HEPA grade), which adds the goal of grading filter media.

If an encoder accepts that B's question is a goal of both A and C, and gives them shared exemplar anchors, the chain
yields CADR ≈ filter efficiency by transitivity. That is a known critical merger: CADR depends on airflow as well as
efficiency.

A careful application avoids the merger, because the anchors (chamber decay test versus filter test rig) and the
bearers (devices versus filter media) differ, which breaks A. The rule's safety therefore rests on the encoder
assessing A correctly, and the run showed that A and K are the largest sources of discretion.

| Identity verdict with extension relation | **Conflated.** One table lists "same", "broader/narrower", "overlapping", "lineage", "homonymous" and "indeterminate" as alternatives, with no precedence | **Separated.** Verdict, extension relation and structural relations are three independent outputs |
| Identity with shared mechanism | no named relation in either version. Expressible as contents: both concepts' models instantiate one mechanism (D7, 5.5) | the same. "Common abstract structure" partly covers it |

### 4.4 Do coexisting definitions have to be identified to reuse knowledge?

**No.** What PEE needs to reuse is mostly carried by relations:

- the **same quantity kind measured by different methods**, comparable only through a validated conversion;
- a **metric of an underlying property** (CADR is airflow combined with efficiency);
- a **claimed value versus a measured value**;
- a **shared mechanism**: vapour-compression condensation in dehumidifiers and in heat-pump dryers;
- **broader or narrower** scope;
- **defined from**.

Each relation can carry its own conditions of transfer (TC1–TC5). Forcing an identity verdict adds no capability for
reuse. It only adds the risk of merging.

### 4.5 Contested interpretations, indeterminacy and duplication

- **2.1** records contested identity explicitly: the holders' verdicts are kept as attributions, the universe's
  verdict is indeterminate, and conclusions are evaluated per reading. **2.0** has only the verbal-dispute diagnostic,
  with no explicit rule for recording a contested case, although holders (D14) make it possible.
- **2.1** makes almost every coexisting comparison indeterminate (the 2.1 run). If reuse depended on identity, that
  would block learning. If reuse goes through relations (4.4), an indeterminate identity is harmless, and the duplicate
  concepts it leaves are controlled: each provisional concept must state the evidence that would resolve it.

### 4.6 Conservative baseline (analysed, not adopted)

- **Strict identity** only for the same operational or deferential anchor under the same definitional conditions, for
  example "CADR (GB/T 18801-2015)" recorded twice. This is an equivalence relation, provided anchor identity is exact.
- **Everything else** is expressed through evidence-backed relations (4.4), each with its scope and transfer
  conditions.
- **Identity hypotheses** are recorded as attributed contents with standing. They are never used to merge stored
  knowledge until accepted under a stated standard.

This baseline uses only commitments that 2.0 and 2.1 **share**: anchors, standing, attribution, scope and transfer
conditions. It does not use either version's section 3.6.2 procedure.

## Part 5 — Operational implications for PEE [INT]

1. **What PEE needs from identity is mostly comparability and relation, not identity.** In every product case here,
   the useful outputs were relations, each with its conditions:
   - CADR depends on airflow as well as filter efficiency;
   - room size and cleaning time are calculated from CADR;
   - the "% removal" figures have unknown measurands;
   - a sensory attribute differs from an instrumental measure;
   - enzyme activity is a mechanism-level quantity, and stain removal a system-level outcome;
   - a heat-pump dryer shares a condensation mechanism with a dehumidifier, but not its metrics.

   No task needed a verdict that two coexisting definitions are the same concept.
2. **The main risk is a merger.** The decisive errors avoided here were mergers: CADR ≈ filter efficiency, NanoProtect
   HEPA ≈ HEPA, °Brix ≈ sweetness, heat-pump dryer metrics ≈ dehumidifier ratings. Both versions avoided them through
   anchors, attribution and scope, not through section 3.6.2. A rule that can output "same" through a
   non-transitive chain (4.2) is the most dangerous component for a system that would merge stored knowledge on a
   "same" verdict.
3. **Indeterminacy is harmless when reuse goes through relations.** If an executable system makes identity a
   precondition for reuse, 2.1's frequent indeterminacy would block learning. If reuse goes through evidence-backed
   relations with transfer conditions, it does not.
4. **The document set was weak.** There was no dehumidifier, no dishwasher, no independent consumer test and no
   espresso standard. Cross-category learning (G1′) could be tested only qualitatively, and in the reverse direction.

## Part 6 — Answers to the owner's decision questions

1. **Does S0 2.0 outperform S0 2.1 on the tested product-domain requirements?** **No.** It scored three items higher
   (132 vs 129), but two of those are encoder variance in areas where the rules are identical. The only
   version-attributable difference favours 2.0 by one inappropriate indeterminacy. That is too small to establish
   superiority.
2. **Does S0 2.1 outperform S0 2.0?** **No.** It has slightly higher determinacy on identity items (13/13 vs 11/13)
   and clearer outputs: verdict, extension and structural relations kept separate, and contested cases recorded
   explicitly. It also has a non-transitive "same" relation (4.2). On this material neither difference changed a
   critical or major count.
3. **Indistinguishable, or insufficient in different ways?** **Operationally indistinguishable on this material. In
   principle, insufficient in different ways:**
   - **2.0:** an ambiguous coexisting-identity rule, and a table that conflates identity with extension;
   - **2.1:** a non-transitive inclusion rule, frequent indeterminacy, and step 0 misfiring on refuted models.

   Both share a lineage relation that is not transitive.
4. **Which commitments should be kept regardless of version?** The shared foundations that did the work:
   - pluralism by correctness condition (FD1) and the five planes (FD2);
   - attribution before adoption and nested testimony;
   - standing with grounds;
   - negation cases N1–N7 (unknown is not negative), and the kinds of uncertainty;
   - scale types and dimensions;
   - evaluative contents with mandatory parameters;
   - designs, norms and goals;
   - transfer conditions TC1–TC5 with scope;
   - recording conflicts within a source (C12);
   - frames by constitutive commitments (D6), and lineage.

   From 2.1, **as practices rather than identity criteria**: keeping the identity verdict, the extension relation and
   the structural relations separate, and recording contested cases.
5. **Are the identity defects blockers for a limited research prototype?** **Not if they are contained.** They block
   any design in which a "same concept" verdict from section 3.6.2, in either version, triggers a merge of stored
   knowledge. They can be contained by:
   - **(a)** strict identity only for an identical operational or deferential anchor under identical definitional
     conditions;
   - **(b)** recording every other identity judgement as an attributed hypothesis with standing, never as a merge;
   - **(c)** reuse through evidence-backed relations with transfer conditions;
   - **(d)** explicit indeterminacy that does not block relation-based reuse.

   With these safeguards, no identity verdict from 3.6.2 is load-bearing.
6. **Minimum semantic safeguards for S1:**
   - no merge without strict anchor identity;
   - a check of transitive closure on any proposed identity set;
   - provenance on every item, with nested attribution;
   - N1 gaps never stored as negatives;
   - conflicts within a source stored on both sides;
   - quantities stored with unit, scale type, conditions, method and tested object;
   - function and performance kept apart;
   - transfers stored as hypotheses with TC1–TC5 grounds;
   - evaluative contents incomplete unless goal, holder, conditions and comparator are present;
   - values never used as evidence;
   - a refuted model kept as a model with failed fit, not converted into an "eliminated concept".
7. **What remains unverified before PEE can be said to understand, acquire and reuse knowledge:**
   - any executable behaviour (question 3 of 0.10: entirely untested);
   - extraction from full documents at scale, including figures and supplements;
   - recognition across large literatures;
   - whether relation-based reuse actually improves later search, reading and evaluation;
   - performance on the categories missing here (dehumidifiers, dishwashers, robot vacuums, independent consumer
     tests, espresso standards);
   - behaviour on coexisting definitions in the product domain (test standards, competing metrics);
   - independent human review of any of this.
8. **Which basis should the owner prefer?** **A conservative subset of the shared foundations** (the commitments in
   answer 4 and the safeguards in answers 5–6), as a provisional basis for a limited S1 investigation. **Neither
   version's section 3.6.2 identity procedure** should be authoritative. If one frozen text must be named, **S0 2.1**
   is marginally preferable as a reference text, for its separated outputs and its explicit contested cases, **with
   its identity verdicts treated as non-authoritative hypotheses**. This is a recommendation, not a validated hybrid.
   No S0 2.2 has been created.

**Can either foundation support rebuilding PEE cleanly, or integrating it with legacy components, while remaining a
general scientific semantic universe?**

- The shared foundations are domain-general: nothing in them is product-specific, and in this test they handled
  product material from six different domains without new notions. They are therefore a plausible semantic core for
  either strategy.
- The identity procedure is not reliable enough to bear weight in either strategy.
- Which strategy is better (clean rebuild, integration, or selective reuse) cannot be judged from this evaluation,
  because no executable component was tested.

## Part 7 — Limitations

- **Small, uneven sample.** Six documents, three of them tangential to their category (S2 healthcare HVAC, S3 survey,
  S6 agricultural dryer). Two categories were missing, and so was every independent professional consumer test. These
  limits come from the fixed rule and from access controls (deviations 1–4).
- **Internal evaluation only.** Five Claude instances took part (preparer, reference annotator, two encoders,
  designer) and count as one source. The reference is itself a Claude reading of the originals.
- **The scoring was done by the designer**, who wrote both specifications. Counts of omissions carry roughly ±2 items
  of judgement.
- **One encoder per version.** Determinacy was estimated only against the designer's re-encoding of the identity
  items.
- **The identity defects were not exercised** by this material (3.5). The identity analysis (Part 4) is analytical,
  with constructed chains.
- **Results describe what a careful Claude-based encoding under each specification preserves.** They do not describe
  an autonomous PEE.
