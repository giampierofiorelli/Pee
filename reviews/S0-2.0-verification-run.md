### 0.6 Procedure for stratum B

For each case, in this order:

1. **Case summary.** Write a neutral factual summary of the episode or of the coexisting definitions, from accessible
   sources.
2. **Expert treatment.** Record how the literature treats the case (same concept, meaning change, new concept,
   distinct coexisting concepts, or contested), with sources. This is done **before** applying sections 3.6.2 and
   3.6.3.
3. **Designer encoding:** the dimensions A, G, X and C, the step of 3.6.2 or the relation of 3.6.3, the frames (D6),
   the revision classes (6.3) and any reading-dependent conclusions (3.6.4).
4. **Blind encoding.** A second Claude instance receives the specification and the case summary (step 1) only, not
   the expert treatment or the designer's encoding, and encodes the same points.
5. **Rival encodings** (designer): S0 1.0 (core/collateral), P2+ (statements with contexts and versioning, given S0's
   other distinctions), and P1.

### 0.7 Probes on the owner's two concerns

- **P-acid.** The acids case (E2) is development material, so it is **not evidence**. It is re-examined against the
  chemical literature, including IUPAC usage, to see whether the 2.0 reading ("one concept with frame-relative roles")
  hides a scientifically relevant distinction. **Failure:** if the literature uses the definitions as distinct
  concepts *and* S0 2.0 cannot represent a difference that matters to the conclusions, this counts as an FC4-type
  failure.
- **P-asym.** In every stratum B case with a goal *added* or *dropped*, record:
  - the outcome under the specification's **asymmetric** rule (addition preserves G);
  - the outcome under a **symmetric variant** (addition also breaks G);
  - which of the two agrees with the expert treatment.

  This is an analysis. Neither rule is adopted or changed by the run.

### 0.8 Failure conditions and decision rule (fixed now)

The failure conditions are **FC1–FC6** of section 9.7.3 as written, with FC4 judged against the expert treatment of
step 0.6.2.

| Finding | Classification |
|---|---|
| FC1, FC2 or FC5 in any case | **Structural defect** |
| FC3 or FC4 in **two or more** of the six stratum B cases | **Structural defect** of the identity criteria (systematic) |
| FC3 or FC4 in exactly one stratum B case, any FC in stratum A apart from FC1, FC2 and FC5, or FC6 | **Local defect** |
| P-acid failure | **Structural defect** |
| P-asym: the symmetric variant agrees with experts in **two or more** cases where the two rules differ, and the asymmetric rule in **none** | **Evidence against the asymmetric rule**, reported as a local defect of FD3 |

**Recommendation rule, fixed in advance:**

- **No structural defect:** recommend approving S0 2.0 as a **provisional theoretical foundation** and starting S1,
  with the local defects listed as items to carry into S1.
- **Any structural defect:** recommend **not** starting S1 yet, and present the defects with their options. No
  corrections are made in this run.

### 2.2 Stratum B: identity and frames

For each case: the expert treatment recorded before encoding (step 0.6.2), then the designer's encoding (step 0.6.3).
The blind encoding and the comparison follow in section 2.3.

**B1 — Imponderable fluids: electricity**

- *Expert treatment.* The fluid theories are described as superseded models, and the electric fluid as a substance
  has no counterpart. Historical accounts credit Franklin with the conservation of charge and with the terms
  "charge", "positive" and "negative". Specialist philosophical sources on reference continuity for this case were
  **not found** in this run. Weakly documented reading: **the substance concept was eliminated; the quantity concept
  continued as charge.**
- *Designer encoding:*
  - (b) "quantity of electricity" → "electric charge": A preserved (Leyden jar and electroscope exemplars; torsion
    balance), G preserved with goals added (source of the field, quantisation), central connections changed →
    **step 4c, meaning change of one concept.** Matches the expert treatment.
  - (a) "electric fluid" → ? The successor is not fixed by the specification. Taken as continuing into "charge"
    (same exemplars and goals; the category commitment changes from substance to property), the result is **4c,
    meaning change**. That conflicts with the expert reading of elimination. Taken as continuing into "electron"
    (different paradigm anchors, cathode rays; shared goal of explaining conduction by a carrier), the result is
    **step 2, indeterminate**. By D3, the reference of the fluid concept is **indeterminate between empty and
    charge**.
  - (c) the category "imponderable fluid" has empty extension in later frames: an L6 elimination at the level of the
    scaffold.
  - Frames: one-fluid theory → field theory, a frame succession (L5). The torsion-balance method's presupposition of
    a conserved quantity is kept across the succession.
  - *Discretion that changes the outcome:* (1) the choice of successor for an eliminated substance concept; (2)
    whether a change of category commitment in a descriptive anchor breaks A or is a change of central connection.

**B2 — Classical elements → chemical element**

- *Expert treatment.* Aristotle's elements are treated as a different notion: they transmute, and are not components
  that survive chemical change. Hendry ("Lavoisier and Mendeleev on the elements", 2005) holds that Lavoisier's,
  Mendeleev's and the IUPAC definitions differ explicitly but share an underlying notion of element, the component of
  compounds that survives chemical change. Reading: **Aristotle → Lavoisier, distinct concepts; Lavoisier →
  Mendeleev → atomic number, continuity with revision** (mildly contested).
- *Designer encoding:*
  - Aristotle → Lavoisier: A broken (paradigm cases earth, water, air, fire versus oxygen, metals, sulfur; no shared
    operational anchor). G: the goal of explaining sensible qualities and transmutation is dropped, so G is broken →
    **step 1, new concept.** Matches. *Discretion:* if goals are taken coarsely ("what are bodies composed of?"), G is
    preserved and the outcome is **step 2, indeterminate**.
  - Lavoisier → Mendeleev → atomic number: A preserved (paradigm elements jointly in force; lime, light and caloric
    reclassified; operational anchor replaced by atomic weight, then atomic number, agreeing on most cases). G
    preserved, with goals added (periodicity, structure). Central connection changed; extension changed beyond
    borderline cases → **step 4c, meaning change.** Matches.
  - Frames: Aristotelian natural philosophy → Lavoisier's chemistry. This is a frame succession: the gravimetric
    method presupposes conservation of mass, which is constitutive under D6.

**B3 — Dalton's indivisible atom**

- *Expert treatment.* Historical and textbook accounts treat the episode as a radical modification of Dalton's theory,
  with the term "atom" retained for the same entities. Specialist sources on reference continuity were **not found**
  in this run. Weakly documented reading: **same concept, revised.**
- *Designer encoding:* A preserved (combining proportions and atomic weights; exemplar atoms of H and O). G preserved
  (combining proportions, conservation in reactions), with goals added (spectra, radioactivity). Central connections
  changed ("indivisible", "identical within an element") → **step 4c, meaning change.** Matches.
- *Frames:* by D6, indivisibility was **not presupposed** by the methods for determining atomic weights. It was a
  theoretical content, later refuted (L2/L4). There was no frame succession at that point. D6 gives a determinate
  answer here.

**B4 — Life (coexisting definitions)**

- *Expert treatment.* Contested. Machery (2012) holds that the project of defining life is either impossible (folk
  concept) or pointless (scientific definitions). Cleland and Chyba (2002) and Cleland (2012) hold that a definition is
  premature. Others defend working definitions. Reading: **contested; no agreed answer on whether the definitions
  express one concept.**
- *Designer encoding:* coexisting frames (astrobiology, autopoietic theory, biology teaching, thermodynamics). The
  paradigm exemplars (organisms) are shared, so A is shared. G: there is a shared umbrella question ("what
  distinguishes living from non-living systems?") and specific goals differ by frame. Under the asymmetric rule,
  specific goals count as additions, so the result is **"same concept, frame-relative roles" (3.6.3)**, with
  frame-relative extensions diverging on borderline cases. "Viruses are alive" is **reading-dependent** (3.6.4).
  *Discretion that changes the outcome:* if the specific goals are taken as *the* goals, they differ and the result is
  **distinct, overlapping concepts**. The specification does not fix the granularity of goals.
- *Against the expert treatment:* S0 gives a **determinate** identity verdict where the literature is contested.
  This is not a preregistered failure condition (see Part 3).

**B5 — Chemical element (coexisting senses)**

- *Expert treatment.* IUPAC keeps a double definition. Paneth's "basic substance" and "simple substance" are widely
  treated as **two related senses**: complementary for some authors, confusing for others.
- *Designer encoding:* same frame (chemistry); A differs (constituent persisting in compounds versus samples of free
  substance, including allotropes); G differs (composition and periodic classification versus properties of free
  substances); extensions are of different categories (a species of atoms versus portions of substance) → **distinct
  concepts sharing a lexical anchor, related by a content** (a simple substance consists of atoms of the element).
  Matches "two senses".
- *Note:* the relation table of 3.6.3 has no named relation for "related by composition". The relation is expressed
  by a content, which is adequate but not named.

**B6 — Probability (coexisting interpretations)**

- *Expert treatment.* Carnap (1945) distinguishes two concepts (probability1, degree of confirmation; probability2,
  relative frequency). Lewis distinguishes credence from chance and links them by the Principal Principle. Frequency
  and propensity accounts are rival analyses of physical probability. Reading: **credence and chance are distinct
  concepts sharing a mathematical structure; the rival analyses of chance are contested.**
- *Designer encoding:*
  - credence versus chance: goals differ (rational belief and decision versus prediction of stable frequencies);
    anchors differ (betting behaviour versus long-run frequencies and chance set-ups); extensions are of different
    categories (holder-side versus world-side, consistent with S0 4.9 and 4.11) → **distinct concepts**, both
    instantiating one abstract structure (Kolmogorov).
  - frequency versus propensity: shared exemplars (coins, decay) and goals → **same concept (chance), rival
    frame-relative roles**, with extensions diverging on single cases. This is reading-dependent for single-case
    chances.
  - Matches Carnap and Lewis on the first point. On the second it is compatible with treating "chance" as one concept
    with rival analyses.
  - Frames: frequentist and Bayesian statistics are distinct coexisting frames by D6. Their methods presuppose
    different interpretations: p-values presuppose frequencies, Bayesian updating presupposes credences.

**P-acid (probe; development material, not evidence)**

- *Expert treatment.* Contested. Chang describes a "rupture" with the Lewis definition. Thyssen (2023) defends a
  single nested concept. The IUPAC Gold Book has separate entries for Brønsted acid and Lewis acid, plus a general
  entry "acid" that points to both (seen through search results; the site itself was behind an access challenge
  and was not accessed).
- *Designer encoding:* Arrhenius → Brønsted–Lowry → Lewis: A preserved (paradigm protic acids; the pH anchor does
  not cover non-protic Lewis acids, but the paradigm cases remain in force). G preserved, with goals added (proton
  transfer outside water; coordination chemistry) → **4c, meaning change**. As coexisting concepts → **same concept,
  frame-relative roles**, with extensions narrower and broader.
- *Preregistered failure test:* can S0 2.0 represent the difference that matters? **Yes.** Frame-relative role
  profiles, distinct measurement connections (pH applies only in the protic frames) and extension relations are all
  represented. **P-acid failure: not met.**
- *But:* as in B4, S0 gives a **determinate** "same concept" verdict on a contested question.
