## Part 0 — Preregistered protocol

This part was committed before any of the cases below was examined against the specification. It must not be
changed after that commit. Any deviation is reported in Part 2 as a deviation.

### 0.1 Who evaluates, and what that is worth

- The encoder and judge is the designer of S0. Every case result is therefore **[INT]**. It can show that the model
  fails, which is the purpose of the protocol, but it cannot show independently that the model succeeds.
- The cases were selected by the designer. None of them was used in designing S0 (`spec/08-domain-checks.md` uses
  E1–E11). Two historical episodes, H10 and H11, are named as *candidates* in section 9.4 of the specification.
  They were not analysed there, but because the designer proposed them, they carry less weight.
- Bibliographic verification is done by a separate agent with web access, against original texts or authoritative
  metadata records, respecting access controls. It is reported separately from conceptual results.

### 0.3 Questions asked of every case

- **Q1 Coverage.** Can every element needed be represented with the meta-level notions D1–D16 alone? Adding scaffold
  categories is allowed, but adding meta-level notions is not.
- **Q2 Distinctions.** Are the scientifically important distinctions in the case preserved?
- **Q3 Determinacy.** Does the specification determine the representation of the case's key points (category,
  relation between meanings, standing, revision level, lineage)? Or can two faithful encodings differ in a way that
  changes a conclusion?
- **Q4 Work done by T1.** Does model-centredness do any work in this case that a statement-based encoding cannot do?
- **Q5 Rivals.** Encode the same key points under rivals P1 and P2 (section 0.5). What does each lose or gain?

### 0.4 Scoring and failure conditions

Each case gets one of three scores:

- **Pass**: Q1–Q3 are satisfied.
- **Strained**: the case is representable only by stretching a definition, or encoder discretion changes a
  conclusion.
- **Fail**: the case needs a new meta-level notion, forces a misrepresentation, or collapses a core distinction.

The failure conditions are fixed now:

| Id | Condition | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | A case in an area (mathematics, qualitative social research, design, data-intensive science) fails Q1 | AQ1 fails for that area. |
| F2 | F1 holds in two or more of the four areas | S0 is not a general foundation as written. This is a structural problem. |
| F3 | Q4 shows no work done by T1 in at least half of the cases (H1–H11) | T1 is judged organisational. O18 is answered negatively at the internal level. |
| F4 | A minimal pair (V3) can be encoded identically while following the specification faithfully | AQ2 fails for that pair. |
| F5 | A revision case (H1, H5, H6, H10, H11) can be assigned to two or more revision levels or lineage forms with different consequences, and the specification gives no decision | O1 and O2 are judged structural gaps, not just open refinements. |
| F6 | P2 matches or beats S0 on every criterion in Q1–Q3 across the cases | The departure from an assertion-based model is not justified. |
| F7 | A competency question (V4) cannot be expressed | The corresponding requirement is not met. |

### 0.5 Rivals, defined so that the comparison is fair

- **P1, conceptual spaces with events** (section 2.7 of the specification). Concepts are regions in spaces of quality
  dimensions. Events are represented by force and result vectors (Gärdenfors, *The Geometry of Meaning*, 2014). P1
  is given probability distributions over regions. It is given no separate theory of standing or provenance unless
  one is stated.
- **P2, a statement-based formal ontology with probabilistic annotations** (section 2.7). P2 is judged in two forms:
  - **P2-min**, exactly as stated in S0: typed statements over a formal upper ontology, each with a probability;
  - **P2+**, the form a competent knowledge engineer would actually build: statements grouped in named contexts,
    with provenance, time, versioning of ontology terms and argument structure. Such models exist in practice
    (for example nanopublications and micropublications; the W3C PROV ontology).

  The conclusions rest on the comparison with **P2+**. Beating only P2-min would mean beating a weakened rival.

### 0.6 Separate parts of the review

- **Part 1** reports the literature review and the bibliographic verification, keeping verified sources separate from
  sources that are only cited.
- **Part 2** reports the results for H1–H11, V3 and V4, with deviations from this protocol if any.
- **Part 3** examines O18 (model-centredness) and the choice of representing a single observation as a minimal model.
- **Part 4** examines O1 (core and collateral, identity and evolution of meaning).
- **Part 5** separates local defects that can be corrected from possible structural problems, and lists capabilities
  that remain unverified.
- **Part 6** gives the recommendation.

## Part 1 — Scientific foundations and literature

### 1.1 How sources were checked, and what that is worth

A separate agent with web access checked the sources against authoritative records (Crossref, publisher, PubMed,
standards bodies), and where possible against accessible originals. It respected access controls. Several sites
refused access, and those refusals were recorded and not worked around. No full text was stored. The designer
checked about ten further sources cited only in this review the same way. Verification is graded:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **V-orig** | the relevant passage, or at least the abstract, was read in the original |
| **V-sec** | confirmed by an authoritative secondary source |
| **V-meta** | author, title, year and venue match an authoritative record; the content claim was not checked |
| **Cited** | not looked up in this review; rests on the designer's knowledge |

**Results for the 182 entries of `spec/references.md` [VER]:**

- **68** confirmed **V-meta**: journal articles via Crossref, plus publisher and standards records.
- **1** minor error: Turing's paper appeared in the volume dated 1937, not 1936. "1936" is a common convention.
- **1** unverified: the volume date of Tarski's *Studia Philosophica* paper.
- **112** are **Cited** only: books, chapters, theses and standards. No conflict was found, but this is not
  verification.

**Specific content claims (18 checked) [VER]:**

- **Confirmed:** 7 V-orig, 11 V-sec. For example: SROIQ is N2EXPTIME-complete (Kazakov, V-orig); Field on Newtonian
  mass, Weisberg's three idealisations, Bogen and Woodward, Belnap's four values, Stevens' scales and Carnap's firmness
  distinction (all V-sec).
- **Imprecise: the "best deserver" attribution** (D3, cited as Lewis 1984). The 1984 paper speaks of *eligibility* of
  referents, building on Lewis, "New work for a theory of universals" (1983). The "deserver" wording is in Lewis,
  "Reduction of mind" (1994). Lewis's 1970 view was that a term with several equally good candidates fails to refer,
  which differs from S0's reading of indeterminate reference.
- **Partly confirmed: complexity claims in section 10.2.**
  - Cooper's NP-hardness: confirmed.
  - Roth's #P result: only the abstract was read, which supports it indirectly.
  - Dagum and Luby: the abstract supports NP-hardness of approximation for **relative** error. The specific claim

    project rules it must be stated as unverified.
- **Attribution to note:** Kamp and Partee (1995) is cited for the classification of adjectives. Secondary sources
  credit them, but the classification predates 1995, and Partee later revised the treatment of privative adjectives.
- **Nothing was found wrong.** The 1.0 corrections from the internal review (renate/cordate, pH, Hempel for
  statistical explanation) stand.

**Sources new in this review [VER]:** of the 40 listed at the end, 38 were checked (V-meta or better) and 2 are
Cited only. Abstracts or originals were read for Halvorson
2012, Lutz 2017, Leonelli 2019, Sloman, Love and Ahn 1998, McAllister 1997, Jumper and others 2021 and Martin and
others 2019. Correction: Appel and Haken's proof is published in **two papers** (Part I by Appel and Haken; Part II by
Appel, Haken and Koch, 1977).

**Limit.** The checks show that the cited works exist and that, for the 18 claims checked, they say roughly what S0

## Part 2 — Results of the preregistered checks [INT]

All results in this part are internal evaluations by the designer. There were no deviations from the protocol, with
one addition: an exploratory set of minimal pairs, which was **not preregistered** and is labelled as such
(section 2.4).

### 2.1 Held-out cases

**H1 — Euler's formula and the concept *polyhedron* (mathematics). Score: Strained. F5 met.**

- *Q1.* The case can be represented. The theorem is a general content over an abstract kind (D10). Cauchy's proof is
  a deductive inference (5.7) that serves as ground G2. The counterexamples are exemplars, such as the "picture
  frame" polyhedron with a tunnel and the nested cube. Lakatos's responses map onto revision levels: *exception
  barring* restricts the theorem (L2); *monster barring* and concept stretching change the concept (L4); *lemma
  incorporation* introduces proof-generated concepts by definition (M1).
- *Q3 fails.* S0 cannot decide between two faithful readings that have different consequences:
  - (a) the pre-Lakatosian concept was **vague**, and the counterexamples forced a refinement, so the original
    theorem was **indeterminate**;
  - (b) the concept had a determinate core ("a solid bounded by polygons"), the counterexamples satisfied it, and so
    the theorem was **false** and was restricted.

  The specification gives no criterion for telling a vague core that is sharpened from a determinate core that is
  revised. This is the O1 problem, and here it changes a truth-value verdict.
- *Q4.* T1 does no work. There is no target system, and mathematical theory is carried by definitions, theorems and
  proofs, not by models of targets. D8 ("part of the world") does not even cover mathematical domains. They fit
  only through the scaffold category *abstract structure*.
- *Q5.* P2+ represents the case as statements plus versioned definitions, with the same indeterminacy and nothing
  lost. P1 is weak here: discrete combinatorial structure is not naturally a region in a quality space, and proofs
  are absent.

**H2 — The Four Colour Theorem (mathematics). Score: Pass, with a local gap.**

- The separation between content and standing works well. The content is a mathematical necessity. The standing is
  "accepted", with grounds made of a deductive inference executed partly by a program. The reliability of program and
  hardware is a methodological content (7.3), which captures the debate over whether the proof rests on empirical
  premises (Tymoczko, 1979). Gonthier's machine-checked proof is a second, independent ground, which counts as
  robustness (5.8).
- **Gap:** S0's obligations on the standing calculus (4.9) do not exclude **logical omniscience**. A standard
  probabilistic credence must give probability 1 to every theorem, so it cannot represent rational uncertainty about
  an unproved or unchecked mathematical claim (Garber, 1983).
- *Q4:* no work by T1. *Q5:* P2+ is equivalent. P1 cannot represent proofs or standing.

**H3 — Numerical support for the Riemann hypothesis (mathematics). Score: Pass, with the same gap.**

- Evidence relative to background (5.8) represents the case properly, and S0's **severity** property is the right
  instrument. Numerical agreement over very many zeros may not be severe, because a counterexample could lie far
  beyond computed ranges. Number theory has known cases where numerical evidence misled.
- Chance versus credence is kept apart: chance does not apply, and credence does. Again, credence below 1 for a
  necessary truth requires a calculus without logical omniscience.
- *Q4:* none. *Q5:* P2+ is equivalent.

**H4 — Grounded theory of awareness contexts (qualitative social research). Score: Pass, with vagueness open.**

- The four awareness contexts (closed, suspected, mutual pretence, open) enter by **M2**, abstraction from coded
  episodes taken as exemplars. Field notes and transcripts are records (D11). Codes are interpretations with standing
  (6.1).
- Participants' knowledge and pretence ("both know, and act as if they do not") are **attribution contents** with
  nested embeddings (Op9). That is enough for mutual pretence. It does mix the universe's machinery of holders with
  the object of study, so attributions inside the world must be kept distinct from standings held *by the universe*.
  S0 allows this through holders, but does not state the distinction.
- Transferability (Lincoln and Guba, 1985) maps onto TC1–TC5. Credibility and dependability are methodological
  contents. The categories behave like ideal types with fuzzy boundaries, which brings in O5.
- *Q4:* organisational only, if the grounded theory is called a "model". *Q5:* P2+ can represent nested attributions in
  named contexts. P1 has no account of nested attitudes.

**H5 — Looping effects of human kinds (social science). Score: Pass.**

- S0's separation of world change from universe change (C10) handles the case well. A classification practice in a
  community is an occurrent in the world, and it causes changes in the people classified. That change in the world
  gives grounds for revising the concept (L4) in the universe. The two loops stay distinct and are causally linked.
- **Open:** an adviser built on S0 could itself become a looping agent. That falls under reflexivity (O13) and is not
  addressed.
- *Q4:* none. *Q5:* P2+ can represent the case. C10 is a principle P2+ could adopt unchanged.

**H6 — The Millennium Footbridge (engineering design). Score: Strained.**

- Requirements are goals plus criteria (7.4). Design codes are deontic norms (4.8). The structural dynamic model has
  a domain of application that turned out not to cover crowd-induced synchronous lateral excitation. This is a
  domain failure followed by model revision (L3), revision of codes and a retrofit. S0 represents all of it, and its
  insistence on stating domains of application is useful here.
- **Strain 1:** design involves models with a **world-to-model direction of fit**: the artefact should be made to fit
  the design, while descriptive models should fit the world. S0 has no explicit notion of direction of fit. A design
  can be encoded as a fit content under a conative attitude, but S0 does not say so. Two encoders could represent the
  design specification differently, one as a model and the other as a goal.
- **Strain 2:** design rationale ("option A chosen because …") needs grounds for **conative** attitudes, that is,
  practical reasoning. D13 defines grounds only for epistemic standing. The reduction (an epistemically held
  evaluative content, followed by an intention) works but is not stated, so determinacy (Q3) suffers.
- *Q4:* T1 helps organise domain-of-application discipline, but P2+ can state domains as statements. The advantage
  is organisational.
- *Q5:* P2+ is equivalent. P1 has no account of norms or requirements.

**H7 — AlphaFold predictions (data-intensive science). Score: Strained.**

- S0's rule that simulation outputs are records *about the model*, which bear on targets only through a grounded fit
  content (5.2), fits the use of predictions well. Per-residue confidence is the model's own calibrated estimate of
  fit, grounded in blind benchmarking. Low-confidence regions and proteins unlike the training data are a matter of
  scope (6.4).
- **Strain:** D7 describes a model by interpretable components (entity roles, occurrent roles, quantities), and section
  3.4 gives a model's meaning through the meanings of those components. A learned network has no interpretable
  components. Its meaning can only be its input–output behaviour together with its domain. The definition has to
  be stretched.
- *Q4:* the case is genuinely about a model, but P2+ represents "prediction p from model M with confidence c; M
  validated on benchmark B" equally well. The advantage is organisational.

**H8 — Association studies and polygenic scores (data-intensive science). Score: Pass.**

- Association is distinct from causation (5.5). Linkage-tagged signals are associations, and fine-mapping is a step
  towards causal grounds. Multiple testing is a methodological content. A polygenic score is a statistical model with
  a domain of application set by the training population, so the loss of accuracy across ancestries is exactly a
  scope or transfer failure (TC3, TC4). The population reading versus the individual reading is captured by Op3.
- Representing millions of associations each with standing is a matter of cost (O16), not of semantics.
- *Q4:* organisational. *Q5:* P2+ is equivalent.

**H9 — Unsupervised discovery of cell types (data-intensive science). Score: Pass, with granularity open.**

- Clusters enter by M2, with exemplar anchors (member cells) and operational anchors (the algorithm, its parameters
  and the dataset). "Putative cell type" is a privative modifier (Op2). That a cluster is a natural kind and not an
  artefact or a transient state is an identity or existence hypothesis. The scaffold supplies the distinction between
  kind and state, but no criterion for applying it. Matching clusters across atlases is recognition, with
  "undetermined" available.
- Whether a subcluster is a new type is O11.
- *Q5:* P1 is the most natural rival here, since clusters are regions in expression space. P2+ is adequate.

**H10 — Reclassification of Pluto (historical replay). Score: Pass, with a local defect.**

- Before 2006, "planet" was anchored mainly by exemplars, the nine planets. The 2006 resolution added a deferential
  definition with a core whose third clause, about clearing the orbital neighbourhood, excludes Pluto. The relation is
  narrower, with lineage. "Pluto is a planet" is true under the old concept and false under the new one. Dissenting
  holders who keep a geophysical concept are represented by a second concept with its own holders.
- **Defect:** the case fits two lineage forms at once: refinement, and anchor change altering extension. The forms
  in section 6.3 are not mutually exclusive. Here the consequence is minor.

**H11 — *Helicobacter pylori* and peptic ulcer (historical replay). Score: Strained. F5 met.**

- The causal part is handled well. It runs from association to causal grounds: the self-ingestion experiment and
  eradication trials. The history of standing has many holders and a long period of resistance.
- *Q3 fails.* The background belief that the stomach is effectively hostile to bacterial colonisation can be encoded
  either as a **background assumption of a frame** (D6 item 5, revision at L5) or as an **accepted general content**
  (revision at L2). S0 gives no rule. The choice changes the entrenchment ordering of section 6.3, and that ordering
  is exactly what S0 uses to explain the resistance.

### 2.6 Comparison with the rival architectures

| Criterion | S0 1.0 | P1 conceptual spaces with events | P2-min | P2+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage (Q1) | all cases, two by stretching | fails on proofs, norms, nested attitudes, standing and provenance (H1–H6) | fails on frame-relative meaning, attribution, history | all cases, by reification |
| Distinctions (Q2) | strongest: explicit theory of qualifiers, negation, evidence and standing | strong for graded properties and similarity (H9); weak elsewhere | weak: one probability merges chance, credence and support | as strong as S0 **if** S0's distinctions are adopted; otherwise weaker |
| Determinacy (Q3) | two F5 failures; "model or content" choices add discretion | low outside perceptual and quantitative domains | high, but by omission | high |
| Meaning change | anchors, lineage and frames, but core/collateral indeterminate | regions can move; no account of reference | versioning only | versioning plus provenance; no theory of anchors |
| Native strengths | content versus standing; scope discipline; anchors | similarity; prototypes; learning from examples | simplicity; mature tools | mature practice (nanopublications, PROV) |

**Reading [INT]:** neither rival is a better *foundation*. P1 is a component, already absorbed in S0 through T4.
P2+ is a representation style, not a semantic theory. Its quality depends on which distinctions it is given. The
most valuable part of S0 is its theory of distinctions and of meaning (anchored role, content versus standing,
qualifier placement, scope, attribution, lineage). That part does **not** depend on model-centredness.

## Part 3 — Problem O18: is model-centredness substantive?

### 3.1 Finding

**[INT], with literature support: the advantage of the model-centred organisation (T1) over a well-built
assertion-based organisation (P2+) is mainly organisational, not semantic.**

- **The cases.** Every element S0 attaches to a model (domain of application, idealisations, fit in respects and
  degrees, opaque behaviour) can be stated by P2+ as reified statements about a model individual. That is in fact how
  S0 itself states them, through contents of kind *fit* and *scope* (D10). T1 never enabled a representation that
  would otherwise be impossible (F3).
- **The literature.** The debate between the syntactic view and the semantic view of theories is contested. Halvorson
  argues that the semantic view, as usually formulated, cannot individuate theories correctly (Halvorson, "What
  scientific theories could not be", 2012). Lutz argues that the two views, properly understood, are largely
  intertranslatable, so the debate is less substantive than it seems (Lutz, "What was the syntax-semantics debate in
  the philosophy of science about?", 2017). The literature on scientific representation shows that "a model fits a
  target" hides a complex representation relation, involving keys, denotation and the user's intentions (Suárez,
  "An inferential conception of scientific representation", 2004; Frigg and Nguyen, *Modelling Nature*, 2020).
  S0 compresses that relation into "fit in respects and degree". S0's choice is therefore defensible but
  controversial, and it is not supported by an argument showing that it is semantically superior.
- **What is substantive.** Three things hold up, but none of them needs models to be the *primary* bearers of
  content:
  1. Models, methods, records and frames have standing and are revised in their own right (3.6 of the
     specification). This is a requirement of **co-primacy** for non-propositional items, not of model primacy.
  2. Fit is relative to respects, degree and domain, which differs from truth. This matters for idealised science
     (Teller, "Twilight of the perfect model model", 2001). P2+ can represent graded fit contents, but a
     statement-based design without them would distort idealised knowledge.
  3. One model can generate indefinitely many contents. This is an issue of economy and organisation.

### 3.2 A single observation as a minimal model

The choice in D7 ("degenerate models") is **not well justified** and should be withdrawn [INT]:

- **It conflicts with the specification itself.** D11 makes records first-class and about their production event.
  Section 5.3 separates records, data and phenomena. A single measured value is most naturally a **situated content
  interpreted from a record**. Calling it a model blurs the distinction between record, content and model that S0
  otherwise requires (C10).
- **It duplicates a mechanism.** A degenerate model's "domain of application" is the same as the content's context
  (Op6) and conditions (4.6). Conditions end up represented twice.
- **The literature supports models of *data*, not models of single readings.** Suppes introduced a hierarchy of
  models of data for processed data structures (Suppes, "Models of data", 1962). Leonelli argues that whether
  something counts as data or as a model depends on its role in an inquiry, not on its intrinsic nature (Leonelli,
  "What distinguishes data from models?", 2019). Leonelli's role-based criterion is the better foundation. A
  processed dataset can play the role of a model, but a reading is not a model by default.
- **Cost.** It imposes the full model apparatus on every observation, which makes the cost problem (O16) worse with
  no gain.

### 3.3 Fair conclusion on O18

The question cannot be settled fully without independent encoders (V6), but the internal evidence points one way. The
best-supported position is **co-primacy**: models, records, practices or methods, and situated contents are all
first-class, and none of them is reduced to another. Standing applies to all of them. This position still satisfies
the mission's prohibition, which is against *presupposing* an assertion-based model, not against admitting contents.
It is also more honest about what the checks found. Whether to adopt it is the owner's decision, and it would require
a new version of S0 (2.0) because it changes T1.

## Part 4 — Problem O1: identity and evolution of meaning

### 4.1 Is the core/collateral distinction sufficiently grounded?

**Partly grounded, contested, and not operational.** [Literature: see Part 1; assessment: INT]

- **Support.** It belongs to a recognised tradition. Carnap split a theory's content into an analytic "Carnap
  sentence" and a synthetic "Ramsey sentence" (Carnap, *Philosophical Foundations of Physics*, 1966). Friedman's
  relativised constitutive a priori (2001) shows that constitutive principles are revisable but play a distinctive
  role.
- **Contestation.**
  - Quine (1951) rejected a principled analytic/synthetic boundary. Putnam ("The analytic and the synthetic", 1962)
    argued that most scientific terms are "law-cluster" concepts with no defining core.
  - Wilson (*Wandering Significance*, 2006) shows scientific predicates spread across "patches" of usage with no
    common core.
  - Friedman's examples are constitutive principles of whole theories (mathematics, coordination principles). They
    are not concept-by-concept cores, so S0 extends his view beyond its original support.
  - Purely role-based (Ramsey-style) characterisations face **Newman's objection**: a purely structural description
    is satisfiable by almost any collection of the right size (Newman, 1928; Demopoulos and Friedman, 1985). S0's
    non-structural anchors and its rule on category commitment reduce this risk, but the core component, taken alone,
    is exposed to it.
- **Operational status.** S0 uses the distinction for three different tasks: identity (3.5.1, "equivalent"), the
  level of revision (L2 versus L4/L5) and entrenchment. It gives no criterion for drawing it. H1 and H11 show that
  the indeterminacy changes conclusions (F5). **O1 is therefore a structural gap, not a minor open point.**

### 4.2 Telling new knowledge, meaning change and new concepts apart

The literature offers criteria that S0 does not use. They are listed here as **options for the owner**, not as
components added to S0:

| Option | Criterion | Source | Strength / weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **O1-a** Anchor and epistemic-goal continuity | A concept stays the same through change if its anchors and its **epistemic goal** (what it is used to explain or infer) are preserved. Meaning has changed if the inferential role changes while the goal is preserved. A new concept is created when the goal changes. | Brigandt, "The epistemic goal of a concept", 2010 | Explains rational meaning change, as with "gene". "Goal" needs its own individuation. |
| **O1-b** Graded centrality instead of a binary core | Features are more or less central depending on how many other features depend on them. Change in a highly central feature counts as change of meaning. | Sloman, Love and Ahn, "Feature centrality and conceptual coherence", 1998 | Psychologically supported and graded, which avoids an analytic boundary. It gives no sharp threshold. |
| **O1-c** Verbal-dispute diagnostic | Replace the disputed term by the candidate meanings. If the disagreement disappears, it concerned meaning. If it persists, it concerned facts. | Chalmers, "Verbal disputes", 2011 | Operational and usable case by case. It diagnoses disagreements, not single changes. |
| **O1-d** Taxonomic-structure criterion | Change of meaning is revolutionary when kind terms' relations of inclusion and exclusion change. | Kuhn, *The Road since Structure*, 2000 | Precise for kind terms, silent for quantities. |
| **O1-e** Topic continuity | Topics are preserved through changes in semantic value. | Cappelen, *Fixing Language*, 2018 | Close to S0's anchors. Topic individuation is itself contested. |

**Assessment [INT].** O1-a combined with O1-b would make anchors and goals carry identity, with the core/collateral
split replaced by graded centrality. This would remove the binary boundary that H1 and H11 exposed. O1-c would give
a test that independent encoders can apply. These options are not yet compared on held-out cases. Choosing between
them is a decision for a revision of S0.

### 4.3 Avoiding duplicates and unjustified changes of identity

- **Unjustified changes of identity** are well guarded against in S0. Recognition has standing, "undetermined" is
  available, and merging without grounds violates C11.
- **Duplication** is not guarded against. Provisional placeholders (M6) and "undetermined" links can multiply, and
  nothing specifies when they must be resolved, or by which evidence. A criterion such as O1-a or O1-c would also
  give a basis for resolving them.

### 5.3 What held up [INT]

These elements worked across all four new areas, with no failure. They are the strongest candidates to survive any
revision:

- the separation of **content and standing**, and of the world-side and holder-side readings of qualifiers (4.11);
- **attribution before adoption**;
- the negation cases N1–N7 and the four informational states;
- the kinds of uncertainty, including the separation of chance and credence;
- the properties of evidence, especially **severity** and **independence**;
- **scope and transfer conditions** (TC1–TC5);
- **anchors**, including the category-commitment rule;
- **lineage**, once LD6 is fixed;
- the separation of **world change and universe change** (C10).

### 5.4 Capabilities not yet verified

None of the following has been verified by anyone, independently or otherwise:

1. **Independent adequacy:** no human reviewer has examined S0. No inter-encoder agreement (AQ9) has been measured.
2. **The rival comparison (V6) by others:** the comparison in 2.6 was made by the designer, who has an interest in
   the outcome.
3. **Acquisition from real material:** interpreting actual papers, datasets and hedged language (O17).
4. **Recognition at realistic scale:** whether anchors and role congruence can re-identify concepts across
   literatures.
5. **Revision dynamics:** whether layered revision gives coherent results over long sequences of changes, and
   whether entrenchment produces the resistance and change observed historically.
6. **Transfer licensing:** whether TC1–TC5 separate good and bad transfers as experts would.
7. **Learning entirely new domains:** shown only conceptually, through M1–M6.
8. **Feasibility of the coherence criteria and the cost of explicitness** (O16): this depends on S1.
9. **Support for the adviser** (R15): goals, evaluation and practical reasoning have not been tested on real advice
   cases.

## Part 6 — Recommendation

**Recommendation: request a substantial revision of S0 (a version 2.0). Do not approve S0 1.0 as it stands, and do
not reject the architecture as a whole.**

Reasons, with their status:

1. **Against approval.**
   - [INT] Two preregistered failure conditions were met: F3 (T1 is organisational) and F5 (core/collateral
     indeterminacy changes conclusions).
   - [INT, partly supported by literature] Both concern theses in S0's definition of itself: model primacy (T1) and
     the theory of meaning (D2-a). Frame individuation (SP3) is entangled with the second.
   - [OPEN] No independent review has taken place.
2. **Against rejection.**
   - [INT] No case failed coverage (F1, F2). The theory of distinctions and of anchoring worked in mathematics,
     qualitative social research, design and data-intensive science.
   - [INT] Neither rival is a better foundation: P1 is a component, and P2+ is a representation style whose quality
     depends on distinctions that S0 supplies.
3. **What a revision would involve.** These are decisions the owner should take; none of them has been made:
   - (a) replace model primacy with **co-primacy** of models, records, methods and situated contents, and withdraw
     degenerate models (SP1, LD2);
   - (b) choose a theory of conceptual identity through change from the options in 4.2, or a comparison of them, to
     replace the binary core/collateral distinction (SP2);
   - (c) give criteria for individuating frames (SP3);
   - (d) decide whether practical and normative knowledge needs to be integrated into the foundation now or can wait
     (SP4);
   - (e) correct the local defects LD1–LD11.
4. **Before a revised S0 is approved,** the review should be independent. The minimum would be:
   - two or three human reviewers outside this project, covering philosophy of science, knowledge representation or
     ontology engineering, and at least one working scientist;
   - held-out cases chosen by the owner or by those reviewers, with criteria fixed in advance;
   - an encoding comparison with P2+ (V6) done by people with no interest in S0's outcome.

The guiding criterion stated by the owner is to find the best possible foundation, not to save S0 1.0. On that
criterion, the internal evidence suggests that the best available foundation is **a revised S0**. It would keep the
theory of distinctions, anchors, standing and lineage, which held up. It would be neutral, or co-primary, on the
organising unit. And it would rest on a better-founded theory of conceptual identity. This conclusion is an internal
assessment and must be confirmed independently.
