## Part 0 — Preregistered protocol

This part was committed before any of the cases below was examined against the specification. It must not be
changed after that commit. Any deviation is reported in Part 2 as a deviation.

### 0.1 Who evaluates, and what that is worth

- The encoder and judge is the designer of S0. Every case result is therefore **[INT]**. It can show that the model
  fails, which is the purpose of the protocol, but it cannot show independently that the model succeeds.
- The cases were selected by the designer. None of them was used in designing S0 (`spec/08-domain-checks.md` uses
  E1–E11). Two historical episodes, H10 and H11, are named as *candidates* in section 9.4 of the specification.
  They were not analysed there, but because the designer proposed them, they carry less weight.
- Bibliographic verification is done by a separate agent with web access, against original texts or authoritative
  metadata records, respecting access controls. It is reported separately from conceptual results.

### 0.3 Questions asked of every case

- **Q1 Coverage.** Can every element needed be represented with the meta-level notions D1–D16 alone? Adding scaffold
  categories is allowed, but adding meta-level notions is not.
- **Q2 Distinctions.** Are the scientifically important distinctions in the case preserved?
- **Q3 Determinacy.** Does the specification determine the representation of the case's key points (category,
  relation between meanings, standing, revision level, lineage)? Or can two faithful encodings differ in a way that
  changes a conclusion?
- **Q4 Work done by T1.** Does model-centredness do any work in this case that a statement-based encoding cannot do?
- **Q5 Rivals.** Encode the same key points under rivals P1 and P2 (section 0.5). What does each lose or gain?

### 0.4 Scoring and failure conditions

Each case gets one of three scores:

- **Pass**: Q1–Q3 are satisfied.
- **Strained**: the case is representable only by stretching a definition, or encoder discretion changes a
  conclusion.
- **Fail**: the case needs a new meta-level notion, forces a misrepresentation, or collapses a core distinction.

The failure conditions are fixed now:

| Id | Condition | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | A case in an area (mathematics, qualitative social research, design, data-intensive science) fails Q1 | AQ1 fails for that area. |
| F2 | F1 holds in two or more of the four areas | S0 is not a general foundation as written. This is a structural problem. |
| F3 | Q4 shows no work done by T1 in at least half of the cases (H1–H11) | T1 is judged organisational. O18 is answered negatively at the internal level. |
| F4 | A minimal pair (V3) can be encoded identically while following the specification faithfully | AQ2 fails for that pair. |
| F5 | A revision case (H1, H5, H6, H10, H11) can be assigned to two or more revision levels or lineage forms with different consequences, and the specification gives no decision | O1 and O2 are judged structural gaps, not just open refinements. |
| F6 | P2 matches or beats S0 on every criterion in Q1–Q3 across the cases | The departure from an assertion-based model is not justified. |
| F7 | A competency question (V4) cannot be expressed | The corresponding requirement is not met. |

### 0.5 Rivals, defined so that the comparison is fair

- **P1, conceptual spaces with events** (section 2.7 of the specification). Concepts are regions in spaces of quality
  dimensions. Events are represented by force and result vectors (Gärdenfors, *The Geometry of Meaning*, 2014). P1
  is given probability distributions over regions. It is given no separate theory of standing or provenance unless
  one is stated.
- **P2, a statement-based formal ontology with probabilistic annotations** (section 2.7). P2 is judged in two forms:
  - **P2-min**, exactly as stated in S0: typed statements over a formal upper ontology, each with a probability;
  - **P2+**, the form a competent knowledge engineer would actually build: statements grouped in named contexts,
    with provenance, time, versioning of ontology terms and argument structure. Such models exist in practice
    (for example nanopublications and micropublications; the W3C PROV ontology).

  The conclusions rest on the comparison with **P2+**. Beating only P2-min would mean beating a weakened rival.
