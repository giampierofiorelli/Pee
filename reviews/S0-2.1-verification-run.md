### 0.3 Preparation of cases (by a third instance, not the designer)

For each drawn item, a separate Claude instance with web access, which does not read the specification, does four
things:

1. It fixes the **concept pairs** to compare:
   - for pool H, the superseded theory's central concept and its successor concept in current science, or "no
     successor";
   - for pool C, the definitions or senses named in the list, or else the two most prominent current definitions
     found in its sources.
2. It writes a **neutral summary** of each case: history, definitions, uses, contested instances. The summary
   contains no verdict on identity.
3. It records the **expert verdict** for each pair, with sources: *same concept* · *distinct concepts* · *homonyms* ·
   *eliminated (no successor)* · *contested*. It marks a verdict **clear** only when sources state or plainly imply it
   and no notable dissent is found. Otherwise the verdict is **contested**.
4. It respects access controls and stores no full texts.

The summaries (step 2) go to both encoders. The expert verdicts (step 3) are **withheld** until both encodings are
committed.

### 0.4 Encoding

The designer and a blind Claude instance each encode every pair from the neutral summary and the specification. The
blind instance does not read chapter 8, `reviews/` or the expert verdicts. Each encoding gives:

- the dimensions K, A, G (at the specific and the most general admissible goal level), X and C;
- the step of section 3.6.2 and the identity verdict;
- the extension relation and structural relations (3.6.3);
- whether the case is contested;
- the reading-dependent conclusions;
- the points of discretion.

The designer's encodings are committed before the designer reads the blind encodings or the expert verdicts.

### 0.5 Verdict categories and failure conditions (fixed now)

Each encoder's verdict on each pair is compared with the expert verdict:

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Unjustified merger** (FC8) | encoder says *same*; experts clearly say *distinct*, *homonyms* or *eliminated* |
| **Unjustified separation** (FC8) | encoder says *distinct* or *eliminated*; experts clearly say *same* |
| **Inappropriate indeterminacy** (FC4) | encoder says *indeterminate*; experts give a clear verdict |
| **Unjustified determinacy** (FC7) | encoder gives a determinate verdict; experts say *contested* |
| **Unclassifiable** | the specification yields no verdict for the pair |
| **Encoder divergence, determinate** (FC3) | the two encoders give different determinate verdicts |
| **Encoder divergence, mixed** | one encoder is determinate and the other indeterminate |

A case counts for a category if at least one of its pairs falls in it.

**Decision rule.**

- **Structural failure of the identity rules** if any of the following holds:
  - for **either** encoder, **two or more** of the eight cases fall in FC8 (merger or separation together), or in
    FC4, or in FC7;
  - two or more cases show FC3;
  - two or more cases are unclassifiable.
- **Local defect:** any single occurrence of these categories, and any mixed divergence.
- **Pass:** no structural failure.

**Consequences, fixed in advance:**

- **Pass:** propose provisional approval of the S0 foundation, with local defects listed.
- **Structural failure:** present the result and stop. S0 2.1 is not corrected, and no further cycle begins.

### 0.6 Limits accepted in advance

- The expert verdicts come from secondary literature found within a short search, and they can be wrong or simplified.
  A third Claude instance prepares them. It is not an independent human judge.
- Eight cases is a small sample. A pass is weak evidence. A failure is informative.
- Stratum A of the 2.0 run (generality) is not repeated. Version 2.1 changed only the identity rules, and the
  regression check of the development cases is recorded in `spec/08-domain-checks.md`, section 8.16 (designer only,
  not evidence).

## Part 4 — Classification under the preregistered rule

- **Structural failure of the identity rules.** For **each** encoder, two or more cases fall in FC7. The condition is
  met twice over, independently.
- **Local defects:** FC4 (designer, H3), FC8 (blind, H3), and three mixed divergences.
- **Consequence, fixed in advance:** the result is presented, S0 2.1 is **not corrected**, and no further cycle
  begins.

## Part 5 — What the failure shows [INT]

1. **Lineages: the grain of category K is the largest point of discretion.** Taking the category as "material body"
   or as "particle" (H1), "trough" or "single trough with two facies" (H2), "universe" or "steady-state system" (H3),
   or "field" or "discrete filaments" (H4) changes the verdict between elimination, split, distinct and same. Section
   3.6 does not fix the grain of category commitments.
2. **Model-type concepts are mistyped.** "Steady-state universe" is a refuted model of one target, not a concept with
   a successor. Section 3.6 forces it into identity comparison, which yields "same" or "indeterminate" where experts
   clearly say "eliminated".
3. **Coexisting definitions: the rule does not discriminate.** The most general admissible goal is almost always
   shared, so goal continuity is almost always granularity-dependent and every C case ends as "indeterminate". This
   matched the expert verdicts here only because all four C cases were contested. The rule would give the same answer
   for a case experts consider clearly one concept, or clearly two.
4. **Determinate answers where the literature is divided (H4).** Both encoders found continuity of anchors and goals,
   so the rules say "same concept". Historians are divided on the identity question. The "contested" clause depends on
   the encoder detecting a dispute about meaning. Nothing in the rules makes the encoder check the literature, so a
   real controversy can go unrecorded.
5. **Minor points:** step 3 pre-empts step 4 when A is broken and G is granularity-dependent; there is no guidance on
   goals whose question presupposes something now rejected (H3).

## Part 6 — Limits of this run

- **Low power of the sample.** Seven of the eight expert verdicts were "contested". A rule that always answers
  "indeterminate" would have failed only H3. The run could detect overconfidence (FC7), but it had little chance to
  detect unjustified mergers or separations, or inappropriate indeterminacy, in clear cases.
- **The expert verdicts come from a short web search** by a Claude instance (Wikipedia, SEP, a few articles). They may
  overstate controversy or simplify it.
- **The designer knew the pools** while drafting 2.1, and composed them in the 2.0 run.
- **Deviation:** the draw pulse was moved from 15:45Z to 15:23Z at the owner's request, and committed before
  publication (0.7).
- **All results are internal.** Three instances of the same model family took part. This is not independent
  validation.
