## 2. Method

**Units.** For A-1 on the O-2 sealed passage:
- the 386 wrong or missing elements of the 165 unfaithful items;
- the 17 spurious items;
- the 56 missed reference propositions;
- the 15 adjudicated critical errors.

The development passage (141 unfaithful items, 32 critical errors) is used as a second sample.

**Coding.** Each wrong element gets one **mechanism**: why the reader produced it. Each mechanism belongs to one of
the categories the owner named.
- **Coded by rule** (rules in `w1-diagnosis.json`, applied to labels only):
  - attribution, evidence kind, attitude and modality;
  - most relation errors.
- **Coded by hand, reading the sentence:**
  - subject, object, conditions and polarity;
  - every spurious item, missed proposition and critical error.
- **Sealed:** every element is coded.
- **Development:** subject, object, conditions and polarity are not coded (86 elements, marked `UNCODED`), except
  through its critical list.

**Mechanisms verified, not guessed.** For every negation error, every critical error and each mechanism named below,
the unchanged reader was re-run on the sentence (`propositions.read_clause`). The mechanism was confirmed from the
reader's actual output and code path.

**Generality.** Each mechanism was also run on short sentences **constructed by Claude** from medicine, ecology,
materials and epidemiology.
- These sentences are **illustrations**. They show that a mechanism is not specific to speakers.
- They are **not evidence of frequency**. They are now design examples and can never serve as evidence.

**Observed and hypothesis are kept apart.**
- **Observed:** counts, mechanisms and recurrences.
- **Hypothesis:** that a general principle would correct a class without new errors. It is untested until stage 2.

**Attribution is worse than O-2's F4 suggests.** O-2 reported attribution accuracy 0.985. That figure is
right ÷ (right + wrong), and almost every "right" is a case where neither the reference nor A-1 names a source.
- **Sealed.** Of the **131** matched items whose reference cites a source, A-1 gives the source in **0**: 130
  missing, 1 wrong.
- **Development.** 0 of 166.
- **No A-1 item carries a cited source** (0 of 280).
- **The cause** (verified):
  - The document puts its citations as a block of Markdown links after the last sentence of a paragraph.
  - The reader splits that block into a sentence of its own, which states no claim.
  - The citations therefore never reach the claims they support.

This corrects the reading of O-2 §5.2 ("about half of the cited sources are missing"). It changes no O-2 number.

**Obligation against preference is not measured today.**
- **The case.** 26 matched items have the reference `requires` and A-1 `recommends`: 13 sealed and 13 in development.
- **How they were graded.** The relation was graded **right**.
- **Why.** A-1 represents every imperative as `recommends` with modality `necessary`, and adjudication convention 4
  accepts a deontic encoding for any norm.
- **What follows.** The distinction the owner named ("raccomanda e richiede possono avere conseguenze molto
  diverse") is lost for imperatives. The O-2 scoring does not see the loss.

### 3.5 Consequences for reasoning (modelled, not measured)

**How the model works.**
- The MS1 rules match subject, relation and object **exactly** (`rules.holds`, `_matching`).
- Only `asserted` and `reported` propositions license answers.
- Conditions restrict answers (`conditions_fit`). Modality is not used.

Each unfaithful item therefore has a predictable effect:

| Effect on a question that relies on the item | Sealed items | Development items | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unreachable:** subject, relation or object wrong, so the answer is lost or an uninformed abstention | 112 | 97 | loss (safe) |
| Unlicensed: an assertion held as a proposal, so the answer is lost | 13 | 3 | loss (safe) |
| **Evidence or status answer wrong** | 46 | 26 | unjustified |
| **Answer reversed:** structure right, polarity wrong | 4 | 3 | unjustified, critical |
| **Proposal answered as fact:** structure right, proposal held as asserted | 0 | 12 | unjustified, critical |
| Overgeneralised: a condition lost | 3 | 3 | unjustified |
| Provenance lost: no source (also 65 faithful items) | 67 | 98 | not used by the rules today; independence cannot be judged |
| Modality wrong | 10 | 15 | not used by the rules today |

**Reading.**
- **Most translation errors make knowledge unreachable rather than wrong.** Reasoning therefore loses answers and
  abstains.
- **Unjustified answers come from a few classes:** negation, unrecognised proposals, evidence kind and lost
  conditions.
- **A separate limit sits on top.** In A-1, even the oracle answered 6 of 45, because question terms were not linked
  to held concepts (33 of 39 losses). Translation fixes alone cannot lift reasoning past that limit (W5).

## 4. What general principles could correct

### 4.1 By class

| Class | Observed | Principle (hypothesis) | Domain-independent? | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Negation scope** | 10 el.; 7 + 9 critical; eight mechanisms verified | Polarity is computed **per clause**, never by parity over a sentence. A negative predicate (`avoid`, `refuse`, `prohibit`, `without`) or determiner (`no`, `there is no`) is a polarity operator **exactly once**. Negation in a condition stays in the condition. If scope is ambiguous, polarity is `uncertain` (W4) | Yes: closed class (Horn 1989). Rule-based scope detection is established in clinical text (Chapman et al. 2001; Harkema et al. 2009) | Scope over coordination and quantifiers ("not automatically", "not always") stays hard (Morante & Blanco 2012) |
| **Deontic force; obligation against preference** | Norms 35 % of claims, coverage 0.26; 26 hidden `requires`→`recommends` | A norm is recognised by **grammatical mood and modal force**, not by verb lists: an imperative in any clause position, deontic modals, modal passives, deontic predicates. Force is kept: `must`/`required`/`need` → `requires`; `should`/`prefer`/`favour` → `recommends`. A bare imperative gets its force from its context, or is `uncertain` | Yes: grammar of mood and modality (Palmer 2001; Huddleston & Pullum 2002) | Bare imperatives do not show their force. Their default is the owner's decision (W2), not the reader's |
| **Proposal scope** | 36 + 2 el. sealed; 19 critical in development; 39 in A-1 | Attitude **per proposition**, from its speech act (Searle 1979). Procedural content (imperatives, steps, schemas) under a proposal marker is `proposed`. Declaratives under it keep their own status. A procedure with no marker is `uncertain`, never `asserted` | Partly: the speech-act part is general. Detecting an unmarked proposal needs discourse context | Wider proposal scope makes losses (safe). Narrower scope makes promotions (critical). The default must lean to `uncertain` |
| **Citation block** | 0 of 297 cited items attributed (sealed and development) | A citation block closing a paragraph is linked to that paragraph's claims as **cited context**, distinct from "attributed to". Whether each claim is the source's, or the author's synthesis citing it, stays explicit | Yes: a document-structure convention (Teufel et al. 2006 on citation function) | Over-attribution would create type-5 errors. A weaker "cited in context" link avoids that. S0 already separates citing from attributing |
| **Segmentation and document structure** | 10 spurious, 7 missed, 1 critical, plus the split errors behind 3 parity errors | Markdown structure decides (blockquotes, labels, code, tables, glossaries). A sentence boundary also falls before a quote or code | Yes, for Markdown documents | Low |
| **Part-of-speech guard on the verb fallback** | 37 el., 25 items | A relation needs a verb with a subject. In a verbless (telegraphic) item, no verb is invented and the relation is `uncertain` | Yes | It mostly turns wrong items into `uncertain` ones. That lowers the wrong rate, but does not raise the right-element share |
| **Conditional clauses** | 30 el. | Only subordinating conditionals (`if`, `when`, `unless`, `only if`, `where`, `otherwise`) open a condition. A condition clause is never a subject or object. Prepositional phrases are conditions only when they state a circumstance | Yes | Prepositions are ambiguous; some stay `uncertain` |
| **Hypothetical cases** | 6 spurious | Worked or constructed examples (labelled "Example", "E1:", or an indefinite actor with "promises", "says") are `illustrative_example` (§8.2) | Partly: labels are general; unlabelled cases are not | — |
| Headed-list frame | 42 el., 12 missed | A list under a heading that names a class or an order takes its predicate from the heading | Partly | The predicates ("ranks", "is a research gap") fall outside the 27 relations: a W2 question |
| Relation sense, nominal and adjectival relations, identification spans, ellipsis, source types, hedges | about 150 el. sealed; most development relation errors | **No general rule found.** They need lexical semantics and discourse. Adding verbs and adjectives one by one is the word-list path principle 4 forbids. The fallback is `uncertain` (W4) | — | — |
| Evidence-kind convention | 31 el. | Not a reader error. It is settled by the owner's evidence rule (§8.2, stage 1) | — | — |

## 5. Realistic limits of the rule reader

### 5.1 Optimistic ceilings (sealed passage)

Each line assumes that every error of the named classes is corrected **perfectly** with **no new error**, and that
the corrected items become faithful. Missed propositions are not counted.

| Classes corrected | F2 precision | F3 faithful coverage |
|---|---|---|
| None (A-1 as measured) | 0.350 | 0.298 |
| Evidence-kind convention (owner rule) | 0.389 | 0.340 |
| + negation, conditions, attribution | 0.446 | 0.391 |
| + linguistic (part of speech, segmentation, notation, ellipsis, passive) | **0.570** | **0.487** |
| + relation representation (frames, norms, verb senses) | 0.741 | 0.601 |
| + epistemic status and identification (everything) | 1.000 | 0.803 |

The first three lines are **closed-class** corrections, where rules have a record. The last two need open-class lexical
and discourse knowledge.

**In this simulation, even perfect closed-class corrections leave precision at most 0.57 and faithful coverage at
most 0.49.** This is a scenario for the correction classes examined here. It is not a general limit of rule readers.
- No measurement yet shows what share of a ceiling real rule corrections reach.
- Selective abstention moves errors to `uncertain` without making them right.

### 5.2 Against the alternatives already examined

| Reader | Measured | Source |

- **The gap.** Between what the task allows (about 0.78 agreement) and A-1 (0.34) the gap is large. No cheap
  alternative examined so far closes it.
- **A neutral finding.** The rule reader is the best of the examined readers. It is also bounded well below the
  reliability needed for admission, by the open-class classes of §4.1.
- **Where rules are strongest.** Exactly where the critical errors are: negation, deontic force, proposal scope,
  structure and attribution. These are closed classes, and their errors are unjustified conclusions rather than
  losses.
- **Not examined.** A more capable reader (a larger or fine-tuned local model) remains parked (`DESIGN.md` §7).

## 6. Limits of this diagnosis

1. **Single coder.** The rule-coded classes are reproducible from the labels. The hand-coded classes are Claude's
   judgement, re-derived from the reader's own output for every critical case.
2. **Not independent.** The reference and the judgements are Claude agents' (O-2), with the known weaknesses: evidence
   kind, and judge disagreement on full grades.
3. **The development sample is weaker:** one judge, no conventions, and 86 uncoded elements.
4. **Reasoning impact is modelled**, not measured. No question set exists for this document.
5. **Depth.** One document is coded in depth. The second category is compared through A-1's public counts only.
6. **Ceilings are optimistic** by construction.
7. **Spent examples.** Every sentence used here, including the constructed ones, is now a design example. None can
   serve as evidence later.

## 8. Recommendation and the stage-0 decision

**Recommendation: proceed, narrowed. Do not proceed with W3 as designed.**

**Why proceed.**
1. **The most dangerous errors are concentrated.** They sit in a few closed-class mechanisms: negation scope,
   unrecognised or over-wide proposal status, double negation of norms, and false attribution from participles.
   - Each mechanism is verified in the code.
   - Each recurs across two product categories.
   - Each, except the narrow "but" flip, is reproduced by sentences from four unrelated domains.
   - Each has a principle grounded in grammar and pragmatics, not in word lists.

   Correcting them reduces unjustified conclusions. Any reader would need this before admission.
2. **Norms are the largest claim class (35 %).** Obligation against preference is the distinction the owner put
   first, and it is lost today without the metric seeing it.
3. **Attribution recall is zero on cited claims.** It has one structural cause. Without attribution, independence
   (manufacturer against independent source) cannot be judged in product advice.

**Why narrowed.**
- The bulk of the unfaithful items (relation sense, frames, concept spans, source types) has no general rule (§4.1).
- Even perfect closed-class corrections cap precision at 0.57 (§5.1).
- Pursuing those classes by adding vocabulary would repeat the strategy suspended after O-2.
- Those classes go to selective abstention (W4), not to rules.

**What stage 1 would need, before any development** (owner decisions):
1. **The evidence rule** (§8.2). It removes the 31 convention errors from the score.
2. **W2**, with two additions found here:
   - the default force of a bare imperative (`requires` or `recommends`);
   - whether "ranks n-th" and "is a research gap" need a relation, or stay `other`.
3. **Two scoring corrections for stage 2:**
   - `requires` and `recommends` are compared mechanically, without convention 4's tolerance;
   - attribution is reported as recall on cited propositions, not only as right ÷ (right + wrong).
4. **The W3 scope**, limited to the classes of §4.1 marked "Yes": negation, deontic force, proposal scope, citation
   block, segmentation, the part-of-speech guard and conditional clauses, plus the hypothetical-case labels. W4 and
   W5 stay as designed. W5 matters as much as W3, because reasoning is bounded by term recognition (§3.5).

**What stage 2 can realistically show (hypothesis).**
- **Likely:** fewer critical errors of types 1 and 3.
- **Plausible:** a modest rise in the right-element share, from norm recognition and the recovery of noun-phrase
  items.
- **Not expected:** precision near admission level.

**What would change the recommendation.**
- **To "stop"**: stage 2 does not lower critical errors without raising others.
- **To "change approach" (a more capable reader):** the right-element share does not increase.
